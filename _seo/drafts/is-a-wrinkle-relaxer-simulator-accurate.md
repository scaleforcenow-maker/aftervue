Searches like "is botox simulator accurate", "how will I look after a wrinkle relaxer" and "how would I look with lip filler ai" ask the same thing. The short answer: a good wrinkle-relaxer simulator is accurate about direction and silent about everything that decides your actual result. It can show how your own face might look with softer forehead lines or fuller lips. It cannot know the dose, your anatomy, how you heal or how the result changes over the following weeks. Treat it as an illustration to discuss with a provider, not a forecast.

This article is general information, not medical advice. Treatment decisions belong with a licensed provider who has examined you.

## What a wrinkle-relaxer simulator actually does

A photoreal preview edits a photo of your own face rather than swapping in a model. It softens the lines across your forehead or between your brows, or adds volume to your lips, matching the lighting and angle in the picture. The result looks like a photograph of you because it started as one.

The model has learned, from many examples, roughly how faces tend to change when a wrinkle relaxer quiets the muscles that fold the skin, or when filler adds volume to a lip. It is illustrating a direction, and a responsible preview says so on the image: an illustration of a possible result, generated by AI.

## Four things a preview cannot know

**Dose and placement.** Results depend on how many units go where, and units are not even comparable across products. The [FDA-approved prescribing information for one wrinkle relaxer](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2021/103000s5320lbl.pdf) states that its units are specific to that product and not interchangeable with other botulinum toxin products. A simulator does not know which product, dose or pattern your provider would choose, so it cannot show the difference between a light touch and a fuller freeze.

**Your anatomy under the skin.** Muscle strength, how your brow sits, skin thickness and how your face moves when you talk all shape the outcome. A still photo captures no movement, and a wrinkle relaxer works on movement.

**Healing and time.** The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes wrinkle-relaxer results as [appearing within a few days, reaching full effect in about a week and lasting about three to four months](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/botulinum-toxin/results). Filler can bring swelling or bruising that the same society says [usually fades within a few days](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/dermal-fillers/recovery). A preview shows one frozen moment with no swelling and no timeline. "What will I look like right after" and "what will I look like in two weeks" are different questions, and the image cannot tell them apart.

**How your skin responds.** Lines etched in over years may soften rather than vanish. A preview that erases them completely shows an optimistic direction, not a likely result for every face.

## Why a preview is not a promise

A preview is a conversation starter: something concrete to point at and say "more than this" or "less than this", when words fail most people.

It is not a forecast. No image model has examined you, and none is in the room when the treatment plan is written. In a medical spa, a licensed clinician has to take your history and examine the treatment area before anything is injected, and [AmSpa's guide to good-faith exams](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/what-is-required-of-a-medical-spas-good-faith-exams/) describes what that assessment must include. The phrasing to look for is "possible", "illustrative" and "likely direction", never a sentence that promises a specific result.

## Questions to bring to your consult

The [American Society of Plastic Surgeons consultation checklist](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/botulinum-toxin/questions) covers credentials, training and realistic results. Add these, with the preview open on your phone:

1. Which parts of this preview are realistic for my face, and which are not?
2. What product and roughly what dose are you planning, and why?
3. When should I judge the result, and how long is it likely to last?
4. What will the first few days look like, and when should swelling or bruising settle?
5. What happens if I want less than the preview shows?
6. Who will perform the injection, and what is their training with this product?
7. Can I see before and after photos of patients who started where I am starting?

Write the answers next to the preview. The gap between the two is your real expectation.

## How to read a before/after slider

1. Slide slowly and stop in the middle. The midpoint shows how much of the change relies on contrast.
2. Check what did not move. Eyes, bone structure, hairline and skin texture should be unchanged, and only the treated area should differ.
3. Watch for a global glow. A preview that brightens the whole image or smooths skin everywhere is flattering you, not illustrating a treatment.
4. Look for the label. An honest tool marks the image as an AI illustration, on the image itself.
5. Judge the direction, not the exact picture. Whether you like where the preview is heading is what you take into the room.
