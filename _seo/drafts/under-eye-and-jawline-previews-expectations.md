Searches for under-eye filler and jawline contouring end in the same place: an AI preview on your own photo and a question about how much to believe. The short answer is that an illustrative under-eye or jawline preview is useful for direction and silent on detail. It can show whether a softer shadow under the eye, or a cleaner line from ear to chin, is a change you want. It cannot know your anatomy, your swelling timeline, or the product and dose your provider would choose. Read it as a sketch and let the provider do the forecasting.

This is general information, not medical advice; treatment decisions belong with a licensed provider who has examined you.

## What under-eye filler aims to change

The under-eye area reads as tired for two reasons that look alike: a hollow where the lower lid meets the cheek, and the shadow it casts. Filler is placed to soften the hollow so less shadow forms. The FDA lists [under-eye hollows among the approved uses of dermal fillers](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/dermal-filler-dos-and-donts-wrinkles-lips-and-more) for people aged 22 and older, and notes that [approved uses vary by product](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices/dermal-fillers-soft-tissue-fillers).

What filler does not change: pigment in the skin, puffiness from fat pads, crepey texture, or how thin lower-lid skin is. A preview that fixes all of those at once is editing, not illustrating a treatment.

## What jawline contouring aims to change

"Jawline" is three decisions in one. Jawline definition is the edge from ear to chin, where filler can sharpen the angle. Chin projection is how far the chin sits forward in profile. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) notes that [chin size can magnify or minimize how large the nose appears](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/chin-surgery), so a chin change reads as a whole-profile change. Under-chin fullness is a separate concern with a separate tool: the FDA approved an injectable for fat below the chin and has [warned against using it anywhere else](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-approves-additional-information-labeling-kybella-deoxycholic-acid-injection-warning-adverse).

A preview can show all three moving together. A treatment plan usually picks one or two, in an order.

## Why expectations drift in these two areas

**Lighting.** Under-eye hollows are shadows, so the light in the photo decides how deep they look. Overhead light deepens them, a ring light erases them, and the jaw's shadow behaves the same way.

**Swelling timelines.** The ASPS says swelling and bruising after filler [usually fade within a few days](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/dermal-fillers/recovery), and the FDA says common side effects such as [bruising and swelling usually fade within weeks](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/dermal-filler-dos-and-donts-wrinkles-lips-and-more). The preview shows an imagined endpoint with no date attached.

**Anatomy and skin quality.** Thin under-eye skin can show filler as a bluish tint or a ridge. A heavy jowl or loose neck skin changes what jaw filler can achieve. Neither shows in a selfie.

**Dosage and product.** Product, amount, depth and session count are provider decisions. The FDA advises patients to [know which product they will receive and where it is to be injected](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/dermal-filler-dos-and-donts-wrinkles-lips-and-more). No preview knows any of that.

**The serious risk.** The FDA's most serious filler warning is [unintended injection into a blood vessel](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/dermal-filler-dos-and-donts-wrinkles-lips-and-more), which can cause tissue loss, vision loss, or stroke. One more reason the plan belongs with a provider, not an image.

## How to read an under-eye or jawline preview

1. **Start at subtle.** If the tool offers subtle, typical and enhanced versions, judge subtle first. These changes read larger in life than on screen.
2. **Compare front and profile.** Jawline and chin changes are profile changes, under-eye changes are front-on, and one view is half the story.
3. **Check what stayed still.** Eyes, nose, lips, hairline and skin texture should be unchanged. Smoother skin everywhere means the model flattered the photo.
4. **Look for the label.** The image should say it is an AI illustration, on the image itself.
5. **Decide on direction only.** "Less shadow here" or "a cleaner edge there" is what you take into the room.

## Before your consult: a checklist to hand out

- A "before" photo in everyday light, front and profile, no filter.
- The preview at its most subtle setting, with the AI-illustration label visible.
- One sentence on what bothers you: shadow, hollow, soft jaw, weak chin, or under-chin fullness.
- Your history: previous filler, where and when, any reaction, and any eye or jaw surgery.
- The questions below.

Questions, adapted from the [ASPS consultation checklist](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/dermal-fillers/questions):

1. Which parts of this preview are realistic for my face, and which are not?
2. Is my under-eye concern a hollow, pigment, puffiness or texture, and which does filler help?
3. For the jaw: definition, chin projection, under-chin fullness, or a mix, and in what order?
4. Which product, roughly how much, and is it approved for this area?
5. What will the first week look like, and when should I judge the result?
6. What are the warning signs of a problem, and how do you handle complications?
7. What are my options if I want less than the preview shows?

The gap between the answers and the preview is your real expectation.
