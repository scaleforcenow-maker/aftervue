A 30-minute staff training on AI previews works when every person leaves able to do four things: say the consent line from memory, run the iPad flow in Guided Access unaided, answer "is this exactly what I'll look like" the way the injector would, and state what the app never does. It is written for an owner or lead injector training front desk and providers together, one practice-owned iPad in the room. The card and the check copy as is. This is operational guidance, not medical or legal advice, so have counsel review any consent language you adopt.

## The 30-minute staff training on AI previews, minute by minute

**0:00 to 0:03. Why the practice uses previews.** Patients ask what a treatment might look like on them. A photoreal, illustrative AI preview on their own photo turns that question into a conversation about direction and intensity. The injector still owns the recommendation. Have the room repeat that.

**0:03 to 0:08. The consent step and the exact words.** Consent comes before the camera opens, every time, in the same words:

> "We can show you an AI preview on your own photo. It is an illustration of a direction, not a result. The photo is used only to make the preview and is not kept. Would you like to see one?"

If the patient says no, the consult continues without a preview. Each person says it twice. A fuller script and written consent are in our consent-script article.

**0:08 to 0:16. Hands on: the iPad flow in kiosk mode.** Guided Access locks the iPad to one app. Apple's [support page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/ht202612) gives the steps: Settings, Accessibility, Guided Access, turn it on, set a passcode. Open the consult app and triple-click the top button (the Home button on older iPads) to start. Triple-click and enter the passcode to end. Everyone runs one pass: consent, capture, preview, slider, handoff.

**0:16 to 0:21. What "illustrative" means.** The preview shows a possible direction on this face, not healing, product behavior, or individual response. When a patient asks "is this exactly what I'll look like," answer:

> "No. It is an illustration of a direction on your own face. Your actual result depends on your anatomy, the treatment, and how you heal. Your injector will tell you what is realistic for you."

**0:21 to 0:24. What never happens.** The app stores no photos. It produces no scores or ratings of faces. It books nothing, because booking happens in the practice's own system. Staff say all three unprompted.

**0:24 to 0:27. Troubleshooting.** If the preview is slow, wait, keep talking, and do not retake the photo. If the Wi-Fi is down, finish the consult verbally and offer the try-it page later. If camera access is declined on the iPad, check Settings, Privacy and Security, Camera for the app, or Settings, Safari, Camera when the preview runs in the browser. If the patient declines, the camera stays shut.

**0:27 to 0:30. Logging and handoff.** The app keeps no record of the consult, so the practice does. Chart that a preview was shown and discussed, and book the next visit in the practice's own scheduler. Hand out the check, due today.

## Ten-question comprehension check

1. When is the consent line said, and what are its four parts?
2. A patient says no to the preview. What happens next?
3. Which Settings menus turn on Guided Access?
4. How do you start a Guided Access session, and how do you end it?
5. A patient asks, "Is this exactly me?" What do you say?
6. Name three things the preview cannot show.
7. Where do the patient's photos go after the preview?
8. Does the app rate or score faces?
9. The preview is slow. What do you do, and what do you not do?
10. Where is the next visit booked, and what gets written in the chart?

Answers are in the agenda. Anyone who misses the consent or photo question repeats the hands-on pass.

## Consult-room quick reference

Print it and keep it beside the iPad.

- **Before the camera:** say the consent line. "An illustration, not a result. Not kept. Would you like to see one?"
- **No means no preview.** Consult continues.
- **Guided Access:** triple-click the top button to start. Triple-click, passcode, End to stop.
- **"Is this exactly me?"** "No. An illustration of a direction on your face. Your injector will say what is realistic."
- **Never:** no stored photos, no face scores, no booking by the app.
- **Slow preview:** wait, keep talking, do not retake.
- **Camera declined:** Settings, Privacy and Security, Camera. Or Settings, Safari, Camera.
- **After:** chart that a preview was discussed. Book in our own scheduler.
- **Personal phones:** never. AmSpa's [photo guidance](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/5-tips-for-managing-patient-photos-to-keep-your-medical-spa-hipaa-compliant/) treats patient photos as protected health information, cash pay included, and rules out email.

## A 30-day refresher cadence

- **Day 1.** The session above.
- **Day 7.** The lead injector shadows one consult per person, listening for the consent line word for word.
- **Day 14.** Five-minute huddle: everyone says the consent line and the "exactly me" answer from memory.
- **Day 30.** Re-run the ten questions. Retrain anyone who hesitates on where the photo goes.
- **After that.** Repeat the session each quarter, after any app update, and in each new hire's first week.
