Patients ask one question before a camera points at their face: is my before and after photo private? The answer depends on what your front desk says, what your form says, and where the photo goes. This medspa photo consent script covers the first two for a consult that uses an illustrative AI preview, and the self-audit at the end checks the third. Preview consent also stays separate from the marketing authorization HIPAA requires before a real photo goes in an ad. This article is not legal advice, so have your attorney review anything you adopt.

## The medspa photo consent script to read before the camera opens

Say it every time, in the same order, so the patient hears a system rather than a habit.

> "Before we take a photo, here is how it works. We use the photo to generate an AI preview of how a treatment direction might look on your own face. The preview is an illustration to help our conversation. It is not a prediction and not a promise of a result.
>
> The photo is sent to the preview service only to create the image, and the service does not keep it. Nothing is saved to this iPad. If you want, I can show you the privacy policy.
>
> You can say no, and we will still do the full consult without the preview. Would you like to go ahead?"

If the patient says no, close the camera and carry on.

## A written preview consent you can adapt

Pair the script with a short written consent on its own form.

> I understand that a photo of my face will be used to generate an illustrative AI preview of a possible treatment direction. The preview is a conversation aid, not a prediction or guarantee of any clinical outcome. I understand the photo is transmitted to the preview service only to create the image, is not stored by that service, and is not saved on the practice's device. I may decline this preview and still receive a consultation. This consent covers the preview only and does not permit any marketing use of my photo.
>
> Signature and date.

File the signed form with the chart.

## Why preview consent is separate from marketing authorization

Full-face photographs are one of the identifiers the [HIPAA Privacy Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html) lists, so a consult photo tied to care can be protected health information even without a name attached. [HHS marketing guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html) says that, with limited exceptions, a covered entity needs the individual's written authorization before using or disclosing protected health information for marketing. The in-person conversation is not the issue, since HHS exempts face-to-face communication. The gallery, the feed, and the ad are.

A valid authorization describes the information and its use, names who may use and receive it, carries an expiration date or event, and explains the right to revoke in writing. That means three documents. Treatment consent covers care. Preview consent covers the illustrative AI preview and nothing else. A marketing authorization, on its own form, covers real before and after photos in a gallery, a feed, or an ad, and the patient can withdraw it later. One signature line for all three turns a good consult into a complaint.

## Biometric-law notes for Illinois, Texas, and Washington

State biometric laws sit on top of HIPAA and care about face geometry, not just photos. Each is a reason to ask your vendor, in writing, what it extracts and keeps.

**Illinois.** The [Biometric Information Privacy Act](https://www.ilga.gov/documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/074000140K15.htm) covers scans of face geometry and excludes photographs. Before collecting, a private entity must inform the person in writing, state the purpose and length of term, and obtain a written release. It also needs a public retention policy that destroys the data when the purpose is met or within three years of the person's last interaction, whichever comes first.

**Texas.** The [Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.503.htm) covers records of face geometry. A business must inform the person and receive consent before capturing one for a commercial purpose, may not sell or disclose it except in narrow cases, and must destroy it within a reasonable time and no later than one year after the purpose for collecting it ends.

**Washington.** [RCW 19.375](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.375&full=true) excludes photographs, video, and data generated from them, along with information handled for treatment, payment, and operations under HIPAA. Its notice and consent rule applies to enrolling an identifier in a database for a commercial purpose. A tool that stores no photo and builds no database has little to enroll.

## A five-minute consent self-audit

Run it once a quarter and after any vendor change.

1. Read your preview script aloud. It should say illustration, not a prediction, not stored, and you may decline, in that order.
2. Pull your preview consent form. It should be separate from the treatment consent and say nothing about marketing.
3. Pull your marketing authorization. It should be its own form with a specific description of the photos, an expiration, and a revocation line.
4. Ask a staff member where the consult photo goes after the preview. Hesitation means retrain.
5. Open the vendor's privacy policy and find the sentence that says photos are not stored. If it is missing, ask the vendor in writing, and ask whether it extracts or keeps face geometry.
