# A Consent Script for AI Previews in the Consult Room

Patients ask one question before a camera points at their face: is my before and after photo private? The honest answer depends on what your front desk says, what your form says, and where the photo goes. Here are the words for all three, for a consult that uses an illustrative AI preview. This is not legal advice. Have your attorney review anything you adopt.

## What to say before the camera opens

Say this every time, in the same order, so the patient hears a system, not a habit.

> "Before we take a photo, here is how it works. We use the photo to generate an AI preview of how a treatment direction might look on your own face. The preview is an illustration to help our conversation. It is not a prediction and not a promise of a result.
>
> The photo is sent to the preview service only to create the image, and the service does not keep it. Nothing is saved to this iPad. If you want, I will show you the privacy policy.
>
> You can say no, and we will still do the full consult without the preview. Would you like to go ahead?"

## A written consent paragraph you can adapt

Pair the script with a short written consent, separate from your treatment consent. Have your attorney review this before use.

> I understand that a photo of my face will be used to generate an illustrative AI preview of a possible treatment direction. The preview is a conversation aid, not a prediction or guarantee of any clinical outcome. I understand the photo is transmitted to the preview service only to create the image, is not stored by that service, and is not saved on the practice's device. I may decline this preview and still receive a consultation. This consent covers the preview only and does not permit any marketing use of my photo.
>
> Signature and date.

## Why this is separate from marketing consent

Under the HIPAA Privacy Rule, full-face photographs are among the identifiers that make health information protected, so a consult photo is protected health information even without a name attached. HHS marketing guidance says that, with limited exceptions, a covered entity needs written authorization before using protected health information for marketing. A valid authorization describes the information specifically, names who may use it and why, carries an expiration, and explains how to revoke it.

That means three documents. Treatment consent covers care. Preview consent covers the illustrative AI preview and nothing else. A marketing authorization, on its own form, covers real before and after photos in a gallery, on social media, or in an ad, and the patient can withdraw it later. One signature line for all three turns a good consult into a complaint.

## Biometric-law notes for three states

State biometric laws sit on top of HIPAA and care about face geometry, not just photos. Each note is a reason to ask your vendor what it extracts and keeps.

**Illinois.** The Biometric Information Privacy Act covers scans of face geometry. A photograph itself is excluded, but courts have treated measurements derived from a photo as a biometric identifier. Before collecting, a private entity must inform the person in writing, state the purpose and length of term, and obtain a written release. It also needs a public retention policy that destroys the data when the purpose is met or within three years of the last interaction.

**Texas.** The Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act covers records of face geometry. A business must inform the person and receive consent before capture for a commercial purpose, may not sell or disclose the identifier except in narrow cases, and must destroy it in a reasonable time.

**Washington.** RCW 19.375 excludes photographs, video, and data generated from them, and data handled for treatment, payment, and operations under HIPAA. Its notice and consent rule applies to enrolling an identifier in a database for a commercial purpose. A preview that stores nothing is unlikely to trigger it. Your attorney should confirm.

## Staff do's and don'ts

- **Do** use a practice-owned, locked-down iPad for every consult photo. AmSpa warns against leaving images on devices and sending them over ordinary channels.
- **Do** read the script before every preview and file the signed consent with the chart.
- **Do** tell the patient afterward that no copy exists.
- **Don't** use a personal phone for a patient photo, even to show the injector.
- **Don't** text, AirDrop, or email a preview or a consult photo, inside or outside the practice.
- **Don't** post a real before and after image without a signed, specific, revocable marketing authorization.

## A five-minute self-audit

1. Open Photos on the consult iPad. If any patient face is there, your flow is leaking.
2. Read your preview consent aloud. Does it say illustration, not a guarantee, not stored, and you may decline?
3. Find your marketing authorization. Is it a separate form with an expiration and a revocation line?
4. Ask a staff member where the consult photo goes after the preview. Hesitation means retrain.
5. Open your vendor's privacy policy. Find the sentence that says photos are not stored.
