Searching for the best medspa marketing agency now turns up two kinds of answer: a traditional medspa digital marketing agency with a named account manager, or an AI marketing team of specialist AI roles overseen by a person. Comparing them on price misses the point. The useful comparison is ownership, approvals, reporting, compliance, and what happens to the visitor who never calls.

## Who owns the accounts, the data, and the creative

Whose name is on the Google Business Profile, the ad accounts, the analytics property, and the domain? Who holds the raw lead list, the photography, the copy, and the landing pages when the relationship ends?

Good agencies set this up in your name from day one and say so in the contract. Some build on their own accounts and hand over access rather than ownership, which is fine until you leave. Hold an AI marketing team to the same standard: every account in your name, every asset exportable.

## Who approves what goes live, and how fast

Medical aesthetics is regulated, so an owner wants eyes on anything public. With an agency, the loop is typically an email thread, and turnaround depends on the account manager's week. With an AI marketing team, drafts appear faster, which only helps if a human reviews them before publication. At AfterVue, the team is managed and monitored by a dedicated human who approves what goes out. Whichever model you choose, insist that nothing posts or sends without a named person signing off, and ask to see the queue where that happens.

## How work is reported

Impressions and reach are easy to report and hard to act on. The numbers an owner can use are consults requested, consults booked, show rate, and which pages or campaigns those consults came from.

Ask both candidates for a sample monthly report with names removed. If the first page is traffic and followers, ask where the consult count is. A med spa SEO marketing agency should show rankings and organic traffic, but the report still needs to end in consults.

## Compliance review for claims and imagery

This is where a general-purpose vendor gets a medspa into trouble. Three layers apply, in general terms. Meta treats health and wellness as a sensitive category, and its policies restrict how such advertisers target, what conversion data they use, and how before and after imagery and personal appearance appear in ads. The FTC's Endorsement Guides require testimonials to reflect what consumers can generally expect, or to disclose otherwise, and require material connections to be disclosed. State medical boards and consumer protection laws regulate how licensed providers advertise, including superlatives, guarantees, and patient photos used with consent.

Ask each candidate how a before and after image or testimonial moves from draft to published: who checks the claim, the consent, and the platform policy. A good agency has a checklist and a responsible person. A good AI marketing team has the same, with the human reviewer as the final gate, and can show you a rejected draft.

## The top of the funnel nobody owns

Most medspa marketing is built to drive calls and form fills. The larger group is the visitor who reads two treatment pages and leaves without doing either. Agencies tend to answer with retargeting, AI marketing teams with more content and follow-up. Neither gives that visitor a reason to act on the first visit. Ask what each candidate would put on the treatment page for the person who is curious but not ready to call. An illustrative AI preview on the visitor's own face is one answer.

## Questions to ask on the first call

- Which accounts and assets will be in our name, and what do we keep if we leave?
- Who approves content before it publishes, and what is the typical turnaround?
- Show us a sample report. Where are consults requested and booked?
- How do you review before and after images and testimonials against Meta policy, FTC guidance, and our state's rules?
- What do you do for the website visitor who never calls?
- Who do we call when something goes wrong on a Saturday?

## Where a human agency wins

A local agency knows the referral network and the event calendar. It can staff a launch event, send a photographer for real clinic photography, and sit in the room when a provider is nervous about a campaign. When a review goes viral, an agency with media relationships can manage the response in hours. Firms such as Studio 3 Marketing and Lead to Conversion position themselves as medspa specialists and sell this kind of hands-on work. If you need it, hire them.

## Signs of a bad fit either way

An agency is a poor fit when the account manager changes every quarter, when reporting stays at impressions after you ask for consults, or when the compliance process is "we have never had a problem." An AI marketing team is a poor fit when nobody can name the human reviewer, when volume is the selling point, or when the vendor cannot explain how a medical claim gets checked. The tell is the same: the work is faster than the oversight.
