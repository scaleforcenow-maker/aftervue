Good medspa website design does one job: it lets a visitor on their phone decide, in a few minutes, whether a consult is worth their afternoon. Five elements do most of that work. One plain-language page per treatment. A before-and-after gallery with consent on file and honest labels. Booking that is never more than one tap away. Pages that load fast and work with a screen reader. And the element most sites still lack: a way for the visitor to see an illustrative preview on their own face before they commit. Below is a page-by-page checklist you can hand to any medspa web design agency, and a brief template to copy.

## The medspa website design checklist, page by page

Use this as acceptance criteria. A page passes when every row is true on a mid-range Android phone over mobile data.

| Page | Must be true before launch |
|---|---|
| Home | The first screen says who you treat and names the three or four treatments you want to be known for. Booking button visible without scrolling. Preview widget, or a link to it, above the fold. |
| Each treatment page | One page per treatment, not one services page with twenty accordions. Says who the treatment suits, what a first visit involves, what recovery looks like, and what to ask at the consult. No outcome promises. Preview widget after the description, before the booking button. |
| Gallery | Written consent on file for every image, specific to web use and revocable. Each image labeled with the treatment, the number of sessions, and the time since the last session. Illustrations labeled as illustrations on the image itself. No stock models presented as results. |
| Booking | Persistent button on mobile, booking link in the header, tap-to-call link. Your scheduling tool embedded on your own domain. Full flow tested end to end on a phone. |
| About and team | Credentials and licenses stated plainly. No awards wall above the fold. |
| Privacy | Says what you collect, where it goes, how long you keep it, how to ask for deletion, and names every vendor a form or widget sends data to. |
| Every page | Core Web Vitals in the good range. WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Hours, address, and phone consistent with your Google Business Profile. |

## The gallery reuse trap

A gallery built for your website often cannot be reused in paid social. Meta's advertising standards for health and wellness restrict before-and-after imagery, limit cosmetic procedure ads to adults, and prohibit framing that implies negative self-perception ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness)). Build the gallery for the site and plan ad creative separately. This is not legal advice; have your attorney review consent forms.

## Speed and accessibility are design decisions

Google's Core Web Vitals call a page good when the largest element loads within 2.5 seconds, interaction responds within 200 milliseconds, and cumulative layout shift stays at or below 0.1 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). Hero videos, uncompressed gallery images, and chat widgets are the usual reasons medspa sites miss.

Write accessibility into the brief as WCAG 2.1 Level AA ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/)). In practice that means text contrast of at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text ([W3C, contrast minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum)), a text alternative for every image, forms that work from a keyboard and a screen reader, and captions on video. A site that works for low vision also works on a cracked screen in sunlight.

## The preview widget most medspa sites lack

Visitors leave because they cannot picture the result on their own face. A gallery shows someone else. A consult shows them, but only after they commit an afternoon.

A preview widget closes that gap. The visitor uploads a photo, selects a treatment area, and sees an illustrative AI preview of a possible direction before deciding whether to book. Done well, the widget labels the image as an illustration rather than a prediction, never stores the photo, never scores or rates anyone's face, and hands the visitor to your own booking page. It does not replace the consult. It gives the visitor a reason to show up. If you are redesigning anyway, decide now where it sits on each treatment page.

## How to brief a medspa web design agency

Whoever you hire, the brief matters more than the portfolio. Copy this and fill in the brackets.

> **Goal.** Turn mobile visitors into booked consults for [treatments].
>
> **Pages.** Home, one page per treatment [list], gallery, about, booking, privacy, contact.
>
> **Content owner.** [Role] signs off every clinical sentence. No outcome promises anywhere.
>
> **Gallery rules.** Consent on file, labels on every image, no stock models as results.
>
> **Booking.** One tap from every screen. Scheduling tool: [name], embedded on our domain.
>
> **Preview widget.** On every treatment page, labeled illustrative, photo never stored, hands off to our booking page.
>
> **Acceptance criteria.** Core Web Vitals good, WCAG 2.1 AA, tested on a mid-range Android over mobile data.
>
> **Ownership.** Domain, hosting, analytics, and code licensed to us. Content exportable.

If you sell skincare retail, a Shopify storefront can sit beside the clinical site. A Shopify medspa web designer should keep patient forms and photos off the store platform entirely.

Settle ownership before signing. A custom website for a medspa is an asset, so the domain registration, hosting account, analytics property, and code should be yours, with content you can export if you leave.
