Most medspa websites are built to impress another medspa owner. The visitor who matters is on her phone, between two other tabs, deciding whether a consult is worth her afternoon. Good medspa website design is whatever helps her decide, quickly and honestly. This guide covers the elements that do that, the one most sites are missing, and how to brief a medspa web design agency so you get it.

## Start with the decision the visitor is making

A prospective patient arrives with three questions. Is this the right treatment for what I want. Will it look natural on me. What happens if I book. Every page element either answers one of those or gets in the way. Awards, team bios, and brand films belong below the answers.

## Treatment pages that answer real questions

One page per treatment, in plain language, beats a single "Services" page with twenty accordions. Each page should state who the treatment suits, what a first visit involves, what recovery looks like, and what to ask at the consult. Avoid outcome promises. "We will review what is realistic for your skin at your consult" is a promise you can keep.

## Before and after photos: consent, labelling, and no stock models

A gallery is the most persuasive part of a medspa site and the easiest to get wrong.

- **Consent on file for every image.** Written, specific to web use, revocable, and stored where you can find it if a patient changes her mind.
- **Label everything.** Treatment, number of sessions, time since the last session, and any difference in lighting or angle. If an image is an illustration rather than a patient result, say so on the image itself.
- **No stock models presented as results.** A licensed model photo is marketing imagery. In a results gallery it misleads the visitor and invites regulatory trouble. Meta's advertising rules also restrict before-and-after imagery, so a website gallery often cannot be reused in ads without edits. That policy page is unverified from sandbox; confirm the current wording before running paid campaigns.

## Booking that is one tap away

Booking should never be more than one tap away: a persistent button on mobile, a booking link in the header, and a call link that opens the dialler. Embed your scheduling tool on your own domain, and test the full flow on a mid-range Android phone over mobile data, not only a new iPhone on office wifi.

## Speed, accessibility, and privacy are design decisions

Google's Core Web Vitals call a page good when the largest element loads within 2.5 seconds, interaction responds within 200 milliseconds, and layout shift stays below 0.1 (web.dev guidance, unverified from sandbox). Hero videos, uncompressed gallery images, and chat widgets are the usual reasons medspa sites miss them.

Write accessibility into the brief as WCAG 2.1 Level AA, the W3C standard most legal teams reference (unverified from sandbox). In practice: text contrast that passes the AA ratio, a text alternative for every image, forms that work from a keyboard and a screen reader, and captions on video. A site that works for low vision also works on a cracked screen in sunlight.

Privacy pages are not boilerplate. A medspa site collects names, phone numbers, and sometimes photos. Say what you collect, where it goes, how long you keep it, and how to ask for deletion. If a widget sends anything to a vendor, name the vendor.

## The element most medspa sites lack

Visitors leave because they cannot picture the result on their own face. A gallery shows someone else. A consult shows them, but only after they commit.

A preview widget closes that gap. The visitor uploads a photo, selects a treatment area, and sees an illustrative AI preview of a likely direction before deciding whether to book. Done well, the widget labels the image as illustrative, never stores the photo, never scores anyone's face, and hands the visitor to your booking path. It does not replace the consult; it gives the visitor a reason to show up. If you are redesigning anyway, plan where it sits on each treatment page now.

## How to brief a designer or agency

Long-tenured agencies such as Studio 3 Marketing offer medspa-specific design and marketing. Whoever you hire, the brief matters more than the portfolio. Include:

- The three visitor questions, in the order pages should answer them.
- One page per treatment, with a clinical content owner on your side.
- The gallery rules: consent, labelling, no stock-model results.
- One-tap booking on every screen, tested on a mid-range Android.
- Core Web Vitals and WCAG 2.1 AA as acceptance criteria, measured before launch.
- A preview widget on treatment pages, with its privacy behaviour spelled out.
- If you sell skincare retail, a Shopify storefront can sit beside the clinical site, but a Shopify medspa web designer should keep patient forms and photos off the store platform.

## Questions to ask about ownership

Before signing, ask who owns the domain registration, who holds the hosting account, and whether the analytics property sits in your account or the agency's. Ask what happens if you leave, whether the code is licensed to you, and whether you can export your content. A custom website for a medspa is an asset; make sure it is yours.
